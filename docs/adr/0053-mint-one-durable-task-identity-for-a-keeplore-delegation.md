# Mint one Durable Task identity for a Keeplore Delegation

Accepting a Keeplore Button Trial minted a task identifier, wrote it into the Delegation Brief as `durableTask.id`, and persisted it only in the Keeplore Delegation store. Nothing ever wrote a Durable Tasks row for it, so the identifier was a Delegation identity wearing a Durable Task's name. The coordinator then put that identifier on the spoken action, and the worker recorded the readback's speech timing under it once playout finished. That append looks its task up in the Durable Tasks table, found nothing, and raised `Durable Task is unavailable` — inside the turn pipeline, after the sentence had been spoken. The caller heard the whole delegation readback, then a stutter and the line dropping, on every call. The same append happens for a response interruption and for every Keeplore status readback, so the fault was not particular to acceptance.

A Keeplore Delegation is a Durable Task, as ADR-0022 defines one: it retains a single identity and state history across the Telephone Session, the PWA, and the Subagent process that executes it. Accepting a Delegation therefore now registers that Durable Task — the Durable Tasks row and the Delegation row are written together, on one connection and in one transaction, so an identity that exists in one place exists in both. Its question is the trial's fixed goal, its conclusion follows its status, and claiming, completing, stopping, and failing the Delegation advance the Durable Task with it. Delegations accepted before this decision are backfilled on open.

The alternative was to route task-scoped telephone evidence away from the Durable Tasks store for Delegation identities, or to give the Delegation store an evidence stream of its own. Both keep the two identity spaces separate and leave the Delegation's telephone history somewhere other than its Durable Task, which is the arrangement that produced this fault.

## Consequences

A Keeplore Button Trial now appears in the Durable Task list and has an Evidence Record an operator can open, carrying the telephone evidence recorded under its identity. Its Delegation-lifecycle history stays in the Delegation store's own append-only events; this decision projects that lifecycle onto the Durable Task's status and conclusion rather than duplicating those events as Evidence Events.

The Durable Tasks table is no longer a Bountywell-only table, and the Evidence Record projection is a union that includes the trial's task. Two of its statuses, `running` and `failed`, do not exist for a Bountywell task, so the operational task counters — which count only `pending` and `completed` — do not count a Delegation while it runs or after it fails. The Delegation's request identifier now shares a uniqueness constraint with Bountywell request identifiers in the same table; telephone Delegation requests are prefixed `telephone:` and Bountywell's are not, so they cannot collide today, but they are one namespace from here on.
