# Use passkeys with offline recovery

Unattended authority cannot be enabled until at least two independently usable passkeys are enrolled, initially the Android device plus another authenticator. A Trusted Browser Session lasts 30 days for ordinary tasks and Evidence Record viewing, while authority expansion, policy and recovery changes, Secret Execution Manifest activation, and raw-audio debugging require a Fresh Passkey Action. Recovery uses offline, hashed, single-use Recovery Codes rather than email reset; consuming one restores authentication only for replacement-passkey enrollment and cannot authorize an Intervention. Email evidence links are opaque locators that still require PWA authentication rather than bearer credentials.

For this personal prototype, “independently usable” has an enforceable credential-level meaning: two different WebAuthn credential IDs, each accepted in its own verified registration ceremony. The relying party supplies every enrolled credential through `excludeCredentials`, rejects a duplicate credential server-side, and directs the owner to enroll the Android device plus another authenticator; browser acceptance exercises two separate authenticators. WebAuthn does not expose a reliable physical-authenticator identifier, and synced passkeys can share a recovery domain, so the service does not infer physical separation from AAGUID, attachment, cookies, or labels. Selecting authenticators with separate failure domains remains an explicit owner setup responsibility; stronger device-attestation requirements would need a separate compatibility and policy decision.
