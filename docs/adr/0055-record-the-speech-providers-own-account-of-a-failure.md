# Record the speech provider's own account of a failure

Every speech-provider failure reached the operator's journal as `LiveKit TTS returned error: undefined`. The gateway's error frame carries a code, a human-readable reason, and a retryable flag; the agents framework's error schema keeps an optional message and a session identifier and drops the rest during parsing, so the reason was gone before the Voice Agent saw the fault. A Telephone Session Fault could therefore be attributed to its failing stage, per ADR-0052, while saying nothing about why that stage failed, and diagnosing the 2026-08-24 outage meant hand-building a websocket client against the gateway to learn that the upstream speech provider was stalling rather than that credentials, entitlement, or quota were at fault.

The speech providers this worker speaks and listens through now read each raw frame alongside the framework's own parse and keep the Speech Provider Error Frame for the fault that follows it. The frame's code, its reason, and the fault class it is classified into — upstream, credential, entitlement, or quota — are reported on the operator's log line, and its retryable flag decides the Bounded Provider Retry instead of the recovery this worker would otherwise have inferred from where the fault surfaced. A frame in a shape this does not recognize still yields whatever reason it carries, a frame carrying no reason at all still names its stage and fault class, and no frame ever raises during parsing.

ADR-0052's rule for what a failure may say about itself is extended rather than replaced. A gateway's free-text reason is a provider's account and is withheld at the recognition and interpretation stages exactly as any other provider message is; its code, fault class, and retryable flag are enumerated tokens the gateway wrote about itself, bounded in length and shape, and are kept at every stage. Only fields the gateway writes about its own failure are read at all, so a transcript, an audio payload, or a caller identity the frame happens to echo is never looked at. ADR-0050's operator log line and its sanitization, ADR-0049's single bounded retry, and ADR-0044's fail-closed ending are unchanged, and the Evidence Record still carries no description of the fault.

## Consequences

An operator reading the journal can tell a provider outage from a misconfiguration of this system's own account without probing the gateway, which is the distinction the 2026-08-24 outage could not make. The cost is that the frame is matched to the failure that follows it rather than by an identifier the framework exposes: a frame is taken once, and one older than a minute is dropped rather than attributed to a later, unrelated fault.

A provider that marks its fault retryable now spends the session's single Bounded Provider Retry even where this worker would have ended the call on the first report, and one that marks a fault unretryable ends the call without spending it. The bound itself is unchanged — at most one retry per Telephone Session — so a provider that keeps failing still reaches the prerecorded response and the end of the call.
